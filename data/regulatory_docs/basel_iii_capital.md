# Basel III — Capital Adequacy Framework (Condensed Summary)

> **Source note**: Paraphrased summary of publicly known Basel III capital
> requirements (BCBS, Basel Committee on Banking Supervision), condensed for this
> demonstration project. Not a verbatim reproduction of the BCBS framework
> documents — consult bis.org/bcbs for the authoritative text.

## Section 1 — Minimum Capital Requirements

### 1.1 Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)
Banks must hold Common Equity Tier 1 capital of at least 4.5% of risk-weighted
assets (RWA) at all times.

### 1.2 Tier 1 Capital
Total Tier 1 capital (CET1 plus Additional Tier 1) must be at least 6.0% of
risk-weighted assets.

### 1.3 Total Capital
Total capital (Tier 1 plus Tier 2) must be at least 8.0% of risk-weighted
assets.

## Section 2 — Capital Buffers

### 2.1 Capital Conservation Buffer
An additional buffer of 2.5% of RWA, comprised of CET1 capital, must be
maintained on top of the minimum requirements, bringing the effective CET1
requirement to 7.0%.

### 2.2 Countercyclical Buffer
Ranges from 0% to 2.5% of RWA, set by national authorities depending on
credit growth conditions, intended to protect the banking sector against
periods of excess aggregate credit growth.

### 2.3 G-SIB Surcharge
Globally systemically important banks (G-SIBs) are subject to an additional
CET1 surcharge of between 1.0% and 3.5%, depending on systemic importance
score bucket.

## Section 3 — Leverage Ratio

### 3.1 Minimum Leverage Ratio
Banks must maintain a Tier 1 leverage ratio of at least 3.0%, calculated as
Tier 1 capital divided by total exposure (on- and off-balance sheet), acting
as a non-risk-based backstop to the risk-based capital requirements.

## Section 4 — Liquidity Standards

### 4.1 Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)
Banks must hold sufficient high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) to cover net
cash outflows over a 30-day stress scenario; the LCR must be at least 100%.

### 4.2 Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)
Banks must maintain a stable funding profile relative to the liquidity of
their assets and off-balance sheet exposures over a one-year horizon; the
NSFR must be at least 100%.

## Section 5 — Risk-Weighted Assets

### 5.1 Credit Risk
RWA for credit risk may be calculated using the Standardised Approach (using
external credit ratings and supervisory risk weights) or, subject to
supervisory approval, the Internal Ratings-Based (IRB) approach.

### 5.2 Output Floor
Under the Basel III finalisation reforms, banks using internal models
(IRB) for RWA calculation are subject to an output floor of 72.5% of the
RWA that would result from the Standardised Approach.
