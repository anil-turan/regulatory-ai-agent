# FCA Consumer Duty — PRIN 2A (Condensed Summary)

> **Source note**: Paraphrased summary of the publicly known FCA Consumer Duty
> (PRIN 2A, in force from July 2023), condensed for this demonstration project.
> Not a verbatim reproduction of the Handbook — consult handbook.fca.org.uk
> before relying on this for real compliance work.

## Section 1 — The Consumer Principle

### 1.1 PRIN 2A.2.1 — Principle 12
A firm must act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers.

## Section 2 — Cross-Cutting Rules

### 2.1 Act in good faith
A firm must act in good faith towards retail customers, meaning acting
honestly, fairly and in a way consistent with the reasonable expectations
of retail customers.

### 2.2 Avoid foreseeable harm
A firm must take reasonable steps to avoid causing foreseeable harm to
retail customers, at any stage of the product or service lifecycle.

### 2.3 Enable pursuit of financial objectives
A firm must take reasonable steps to enable retail customers to pursue
their financial objectives, including providing information they can
understand and act on.

## Section 3 — The Four Outcomes

### 3.1 Products and services
Products and services must be designed to meet the needs, characteristics
and objectives of an identified target market, and must be distributed
appropriately.

### 3.2 Price and value
Firms must ensure the price a customer pays for a product or service is
reasonable relative to the overall benefits, requiring a fair value
assessment.

### 3.3 Consumer understanding
Firms must support customer understanding by ensuring communications are
clear, fair, not misleading, and tested where used at scale or where
customers may be vulnerable.

### 3.4 Consumer support
Firms must provide support that meets customers' needs throughout their
relationship with the firm, including when they want to switch, cancel or
make a claim, avoiding unreasonable barriers ("sludge practices").

## Section 4 — Governance and Evidencing

### 4.1 Board reporting
Firms must produce an annual report, reviewed and approved by the Board (or
equivalent governing body), assessing whether the firm is delivering good
outcomes consistent with the Duty.

### 4.2 Vulnerable customers
Firms must understand the nature and scale of characteristics of
vulnerability present in their target market and customer base, and ensure
frontline staff have the skills to respond appropriately.
